CODEPATH*ORG Case study · Summer 2026

Colorado employers wanted AI capability. They got it by hiring students and training them on the job.

13 fellows 9 host organizations Social, public and private sectors 10-week paid deployment First-year through post-graduate Rural and urban Colorado
The opportunity

A coalition with the demand signal, but no way to build the talent

Colorado Thrives, a coalition of CEOs from organizations across Colorado, set out to help its member organizations build lasting AI capability while creating a pathway into work for students in the state. They could identify the employers and they could find the students. What they could not do was design the training that connects the two.

CodePath had the pathway already running. In Summer 2026 the two organizations placed 13 students inside 9 Colorado host organizations to solve real business problems with AI, on a 10-week paid deployment with weekly mentorship.

How it ran

Three organizations, three jobs, no overlap

Colorado Thrives
  • Recruited and selected fellows from Colorado colleges
  • Recruited host organizations and matched fellows to employers
  • Convened the state ecosystem and the funder conversation
Host organizations
  • Paid the fellowship roles directly
  • Supplied supervision and real project scope
  • Owned adoption of what the fellow built
CodePath
  • Program design, delivery management, and reporting
  • Co-delivered the in-person training with Anthropic, who built the AI curriculum
  • Weekly 1:1 mentorship across all 10 weeks
  • Project scoping support for fellows and supervisors

Format: self-paced pre-work, two days of in-person training, then 10 weeks embedded in the host organization with project checkpoints and weekly 1:1s. CodePath takes ownership of the curriculum in future cohorts.

What fellows built

Production systems their organizations are still running

BRAIN Bench · Emaliah Sawyer, Center for Rural AI
Offered a contract role
Problem
The Center for Rural AI was building BRAIN, a knowledge system that feeds curated rural context into large language models, on an untested assumption: that the added context produces better answers. No way existed to verify it, compare models against the organization's own data, or tell whether a new version was an improvement.
Built
An evaluation system that grades BRAIN's answers against real questions and a scoring rubric. She collected roughly 100 questions from colleagues across every role, each paired with that colleague's own definition of a good answer, then built a four-dimension rubric and a harness that tests three frontier models with and without BRAIN connected, graded by an automated judge that staff validated against their own questions.
Result
Release decisions now rest on evidence instead of impression. The system also surfaces gaps in the knowledge base, so development effort goes where the weaknesses actually are, and the value case for BRAIN can be made with a model-by-model comparison rather than anecdotes.
Avie · Yesenia Mendoza, AdvanceEDU
Offered a contract role
Problem
Success Coaches were spending 3 to 5 hours a week walking students through the same repetitive financial aid steps, one student at a time. That time came directly out of the one-on-one coaching that changes whether a student stays enrolled.
Built
An always-on student support assistant that verifies a student's identity, pulls their school, degree program, and assigned coach, and guides them through financial aid, career exploration, and campus resources. It is deliberately built to ask guiding questions rather than supply answers. Every response is grounded in AdvanceEDU's own knowledge base, with explicit guardrails on sensitive topics and automated evaluations run against dozens of simulated student conversations.
Result
Coaching hours return to coaching. The system also detects crisis and high-risk language and is built to notify a Success Coach immediately, and students get a lower-stakes place to ask the questions they hesitate to bring to a person.
Production pipeline · Yaseer Sabir, Colorado Thrives
Offered a contract role
Problem
A recurring production task was consuming 4 to 6 hours of staff time each cycle.
Built
An agentic AI pipeline that runs the task end to end.
Result
Production time fell to 15 minutes, a reduction of roughly 95%.

Fellow projects clustered into four business use cases: process automation and efficiency, data analysis and insights, knowledge management, and quality assurance. Every project from the cohort is documented on the public showcase.⁴

A first cohort producing this level of repeat demand is the signal worth acting on.³

What hosts asked us to add

Half the responding supervisors independently asked for the same thing: training on how to evaluate AI solutions. In the mid-program survey, only 2 of 11 supervisors said they could fully explain their fellow's project to someone else, while 10 of 11 were confident the work mattered.²

Employers can identify the value of AI work well before they can assess it. Closing that gap is the difference between a fellow's project surviving their departure and quietly going unused.

The economics

Host organizations paid directly for the program and for their fellows' salaries. This was not a philanthropically subsidized placement. Employers put their own budget behind a student they had not met, working on a technology most of them were still learning, and 6 of 6 came out of it asking for more. The model carries its own revenue; philanthropy's role is to underwrite reach, not to keep the lights on.

What's next

From 13 placements to a statewide talent strategy

Scale in Colorado

Colorado Thrives is actively exploring growth of up to 10X as delivery capacity allows, and wants the program embedded in the statewide AI talent strategy rather than run as a standalone cohort.

A public-sector cohort

Conversations with the Colorado state CIO opened the possibility of placing AI fellows inside state government as early as fall, potentially tied to benefits-sector work.

A Colorado Corps

Colorado funders have signaled interest in catalytic support for a regional service corps modeled on the national Claude Corps program, aligned to the incoming governor's youth service agenda.

Peer states

Colorado Thrives sits inside a national network of 30-plus comparable coalitions. The three-way structure here is portable to any state with an employer coalition willing to pay for placements.

What we would build with partnership

Three things this cohort proved are needed and none of the three parties currently funds: a supervisor curriculum on evaluating AI work, continued compute access for fellows after placement, since free-tier tools stall skill development, and a post-fellowship alumni network that keeps fellows connected to Colorado employers. Each is small, concrete, and directly traceable to what cohort one told us.
